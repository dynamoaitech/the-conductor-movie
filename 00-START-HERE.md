## 🎯 KEY CREATIVE PRINCIPLES

### 1. Show, Don't Tell
- The architecture reveals itself gradually
- Carlos discovers it, we discover with him
- Use visual sequences for complex ideas
- Trust the audience

### 2. Characters Are Human
- Chávez: Not hero or villain, tragic figure
- Maduro: Pragmatic survivor, not coward
- Carlos: Complicit then aware
- No cartoon characters

### 3. Fiction = Freedom
- "Based on true events" allows speculation
- The pattern is real, details are cinematic
- We can reveal truth through creative interpretation

### 4. Hybrid Format Works
- Archive footage (40%) grounds in reality
- Dramatic scenes (40%) create emotional connection
- Motion graphics (20%) explain complexity
- All three together = unique voice

## 📝 SCREENPLAY WRITING APPROACH

### Use Fountain Format
Plain text, converts to industry-standard PDF.

**Basic syntax:**
```fountain
INT. LOCATION - TIME

Character does something.

CHARACTER
Dialogue goes here.
```

See `02-script/FOUNTAIN-FORMAT-GUIDE.md` for full guide.

### Target Length
- 90-120 pages total
- Act I: ~30 pages
- Act II: ~45 pages
- Act III: ~35 pages

### Writing Process
1. Scene breakdown first (know what each scene does)
2. Dialogue pass (get voices right)
3. Action description (keep lean and visual)
4. Read aloud (test dialogue flow)
5. Revise ruthlessly (cut anything that doesn't serve story)

## 🎬 THE THREE-ACT STRUCTURE (Quick Reference)

### ACT I: THE DEAL (1976-1999)
**30 minutes / ~30 pages**

1. 1976 nationalization celebration
2. 1980s oil crisis
3. 1986 CITGO deal negotiated (Carlos signs)
4. Trap is built (refinery construction)
5. 1992 Chávez coup attempt (fails)
6. 1998 Chávez elected
7. 1999 Chávez discovers CITGO
8. **Decision:** "I'll use their infrastructure for the people"

**Act I Turn:** Chávez thinks he's winning by using CITGO revenue for social programs.

### ACT II: THE DEEPENING (1999-2019)
**45 minutes / ~45 pages**

9. 2000s: Social programs flourishing (oil socialism)
10. 2002 coup attempt against Chávez
11. Economic warfare, Chávez survives
12. Parallel track: Architects positioning (Elliott buying debt)
13. 2013: Chávez dies (cancer, suspicious)
14. Maduro inherits disaster
15. 2014-2018: Economic crisis
16. 2016: Rosneft loan (more debt)
17. 2019: Sanctions freeze CITGO access
18. **Collapse:** Social programs fail, hyperinflation, starvation

**Act II Turn:** Maduro surrounded by enemies, realizes he must negotiate or die.

### ACT III: THE EXTRACTION (2019-2026)
**35 minutes / ~35 pages**

19. Maduro's impossible position (cartels, people, Russia, China, military)
20. Carlos in Madrid discovers the architecture
21. Secret US contact with Maduro
22. The negotiation: "Only if you save my wife"
23. Chinese coordination (the tell: 3-minute gap)
24. The extraction (theater, not capture)
25. CITGO auction to Elliott Management
26. Maduro in US custody, writing his account
27. Carlos writing memoir
28. **Reveal:** Refineries under new management, same engineering firms
29. **Final twist:** Chinese getting long-term oil contracts anyway

**Resolution:** The architecture was always there. The conductor was always visible. The architects remain invisible.

## 👥 CHARACTER VOICES (Quick Reference)

### Carlos (our guide):
- Educated, formal Spanish
- Technical language early, philosophical later
- Arc: Believer → Skeptic → Witness

**Sample dialogue:**
> "I thought vertical integration would protect us. I was signing our surrender."

### Chávez (the revolutionary):
- Passionate, poetic, references Bolívar
- Genuine revolutionary rhetoric
- Arc: Hopeful → Confident → Doubtful (late)

**Sample dialogue:**
> "They sold our sovereignty for debt? I'll use their infrastructure against them. For the people!"

### Maduro (the survivor):
- Less poetic than Chávez
- More pragmatic, defensive
- Arc: Loyal follower → Isolated leader → Negotiator

**Sample dialogue:**
> "I'm not Hugo. I never will be. But I can survive. We can survive."

### Elliot Reed (the architect):
- Calm, rational, almost sympathetic
- No malice, just logic
- Never seen clearly on camera

**Sample dialogue:**
> "This isn't about stealing. It's about architecture. We didn't create the crisis. We positioned for it."

## ⚠️ COMMON PITFALLS TO AVOID

### 1. Over-explaining
Don't have characters explain the architecture in dialogue.
**Show** it through:
- Carlos discovering documents
- Visual sequences
- Parallel editing (boardroom → refinery → palace)

### 2. Taking sides
This isn't pro-Chávez or anti-US.
This reveals the **architecture beneath all positions**.

### 3. Cartoon villains
No one is purely evil.
- Architects are logical, not malicious
- Chávez is genuine, not buffoon
- Maduro is pragmatic, not coward

### 4. Too ambitious
Remember: Achievable indie film.
- 10-12 key dramatic scenes (not 50)
- Heavy use of archive footage
- Motion graphics for complex parts
- Keep scope realistic

### 5. Losing the theme
Always return to core question:
**"Who's driving, and who built the road?"**
